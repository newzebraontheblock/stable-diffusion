The gap

The focus falls entirely on the baby.

Becoming a new parent is an incredible journey. Yet almost all the preparation, the appointments and the advice point one way — leaving mothers and birth parents unprepared and overlooked in their own recovery.

The cost of that oversight is immense: mental health struggles, and a difficult physical return to health.

You deserve to thrive, not just survive.

What’s inside

Three parts, for body and for mind.

The core

Home Recovery Kit

Clinically reviewed recovery essentials, in the house before you need them. No ordering, no pharmacy run, no asking someone to go out for you in week one.

In every box

Maternal Journal

Co‑created with a midwife and a King’s College psychiatrist. Prompts and guidance for the days when you do not know where to start — the mind half of recovery, included as standard.

Add‑on

Service Vouchers

Physiotherapy, counselling and night nanny support. For when what you need most is a person, not a product — redeemable when you are ready.

Why it’s different

Lived experience, checked by specialists.

Curated by mums and perinatal specialists

Every item and every word is chosen by people who have been through it, then reviewed by clinicians who do this for a living.

It arrives before you need it

The kit comes ahead of your due date, so nothing has to be sourced in the week you can least manage it.

For body and for mind

Physical recovery and mental health are not separate problems. The kit and the journal treat them as one.

For the gift‑giver

The first 42 days

And what happens after them.

Days 1–3
Getting homeComfort and practical recovery essentials for the days when leaving the house is not an option.
Days 4–14
Healing and restSupport for physical recovery, with guidance on what is normal and the signs worth calling someone about.
Days 15–42
Finding a rhythmFeeding, sleep and mental health — the stretch where most people are told they are “fine” and quietly are not.
Day 43 & beyond
Where most support stopsThe six‑week check is not the finish line. Longer‑term recovery, pelvic health and returning to yourself — which is the half we are named after.
