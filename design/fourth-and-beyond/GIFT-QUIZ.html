Fourth & Beyond · Gifter quiz specification

What does she actually need?

A four-question quiz for gift-buyers, and the logic behind its results. Hand this to whoever builds it, or build it yourself in Typeform. Every question here is one a colleague could answer — not just a partner.

The output is not a product recommendation It is a short brief: what to send, when it should arrive, and what to write in the note. That last part is what gift-buyers actually agonise over, and nobody else in this category gives it to them.
1

The questions

Four, plus an optional fifth. One tap each. The tag on the right is what gets stored against the signup for segmentation.

Question 1

Where is she up to?

  • More than six weeks until the birth stage=early
  • Due within the next six weeks stage=imminent
  • The baby has just arrived stage=newborn
  • She’s a few weeks in stage=weeks_in

Drives the timing line, and tells you when to launch.

Question 2

Is this her first baby?

  • Her first first=yes
  • She’s done this before first=no
  • I’m not sure first=unknown

First-timers don’t know what to ask for. Second-timers know exactly how hard it is and want relief, not guidance.

Question 3

How much help will she have at home?

  • A partner around most of the time support=high
  • Some family or friends nearby support=medium
  • Mostly on her own support=low
  • I don’t know support=unknown

The single strongest signal for which voucher to suggest.

Question 4

How well do you know her?

  • Very well — I’d know how she’s really doing closeness=close
  • Good friend or family closeness=friend
  • A colleague, or a friend of a friend closeness=distant

The clever one. It calibrates how personal the suggested note should be — and stops a colleague being handed wording that would mortify them both.

Question 5 — optional, allow skip

Roughly what did you have in mind?

  • Around £50 — I’m going in with others budget=shared
  • Around £150 budget=kit
  • £200 or more budget=plus
  • Rather not say budget=skip

Pre-launch this is research, not a filter. The “going in with others” answer also tells you how much demand there is for group gifting.

2

Result logic

Don’t write twenty-seven result pages. The result is assembled from three modular blocks, so the build stays small and the copy stays writable.

Block A — what to send

Gifts are always the Full kit — never the BasicThere are two: the Basic covers a straightforward vaginal birth, the Full is ready for a caesarean too. A gift-buyer cannot know which she will need, and nor can she. Recommending Basic to a gifter risks sending a kit that turns out to be the wrong one on the day, so this quiz never does. The Basic kit is for a self-buyer making that choice for herself.
IfRecommend
support=lowFull kit + night nanny or meals voucher. She needs hands, not objects.
support=mediumFull kit + physio or counselling voucher.
support=high or unknownThe Full kit on its own. Don’t upsell into a gap that may not exist.
budget=sharedFull kit, framed as a group gift — “three of you covers it.”
first=yesLead the description with the journal; she doesn’t yet know what she’ll need.
first=noLead with practical relief; she already knows.

Block B — when it should arrive

IfLine to show
stage=early“Order when you like — we’ll hold it and deliver the week before she’s due.”
stage=imminent“Send it now. It should be in the house before the hospital bag leaves it.”
stage=newborn“Send it today. The first fortnight is when it does the most work.”
stage=weeks_in“Not too late — most support disappears around now, which is exactly the problem.”

Block C — what to write

Offer it as copyable text. This is the part people screenshot.

closeness = close

“I know everyone’s going to be looking at the baby. This one’s for you. I’m around — for the 3am ones too.”

closeness = friend

“Everyone’s buying for the baby, so I got you something for you. No need to reply to this — or to anything, for a while.”

closeness = distant

“Congratulations. We thought you might like something for you, rather than another babygrow. Wishing you a gentle few weeks.”

Why the note matters more than the recommendationA gift-buyer has usually decided to spend the money before they arrive. What they’re stuck on is the card. Solve that and you’ve done something no competitor does.

The results screen

Heading

Here’s what we’d send.

Then, in order

Block A — the recommendation, with the price shown
Block B — the timing line
Block C — the suggested note, with a copy button

Then the single CTA

We’re not open yet — kits ship spring 2027. Join the waitlist and we’ll tell you the moment gifting opens, in time for her.

Show the result before asking for the emailGating it behind a signup will lift capture a little and cost you more in drop-off and goodwill. Your brand’s whole promise is that it gives things away — a free resource hub, guidance with no strings. Withholding a four-question result contradicts that on the first interaction. Show the answer, then ask.
4

What you learn

This is the real reason to build it pre-launch. Every completion tells you:

  • When to launch — the spread of stage answers is a live due-date curve.
  • Whether the gifting thesis holds — volume through this quiz versus your main signup form is a direct test of your 81% figure.
  • Whether to build group gifting — the budget=shared count answers it for you.
  • How to segment the emails — a gift-buyer and an expectant mother need entirely different nurture sequences. Right now your list can’t tell them apart.
Tag every signup with audience=gifterEven if you never use the other fields, this one alone justifies the build. It splits your list in two.
5

Rules — do not break these

Never ask how she is feelingThe moment a question touches mood, you are adjacent to clinical screening and you do not have the governance for it. Every question stays practical: timing, household, support, relationship. No exceptions, however gently worded.
Do not collect her detailsNo name, no email, no address for the mother at quiz stage. The person answering is not the data subject and cannot consent on her behalf. Collect recipient details at checkout, from a buyer who is actually purchasing.
The result is guidance — get it signed offAnything suggesting what a recovering woman needs is health guidance. Same clinician sign-off as the resource hub articles, before it goes live.

Building it

ItemDetail
ToolTypeform is the safe choice — conditional logic, clean on mobile, connects to most email tools. Interact is purpose-built for quizzes if you want more.
SquarespaceNo native quiz. It goes in a Code Block, which needs the Core plan or above.
Where it livesIts own page at /what-does-she-need, linked from the Gifting section and the nav.
PrerequisiteThe waitlist form must already work. A quiz feeding a broken backend is worse than a plain email field that collects.
SequenceGet the plain waitlist live and tested first. Add this second, as the upgrade. It is the highest-value thing you can build once the basics collect properly — but only once they do.
